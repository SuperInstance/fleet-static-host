## Pass 2 — adversarial (Sonnet 5): the attack

Ranked kid-confusions:

1. **Fan-in is a wall.** Two wires into one input = red-dash, no palette tile
   can combine values. "The most instinctive kid move" dies with no path
   forward. Proposed fix: a Mixer tile (add/any/latest modes).
2. **"Every number changes every tick" fights "wires are one tick old."** The
   headline promise contradicts the latency law; feedback loops jitter and the
   kid concludes "wires are laggy," not "wires are delay lines." It noted the
   engine's own git history (dd9e55c, 2c54d3f, 3df3cba) is adults fighting
   exactly this edge.
3. **Silent no-ops.** A legal wire the receiver ignores: Party-hat skin on a
   rocket does nothing; Grumpy card on the pilot silently remaps to
   cruise/hold/volley/cool because the pilot reuses the villager's bias keys.
   "Wiring is random" is the wrong lesson, and M3 is violated in spirit.

Unbuildable games → fabric edges (the map):

- **Platformer** — missing tiles (gravity, a vy/impulse input, landing events)
  plus a law patch: clamping is currently a hidden state change (N2 brush).
- **Two-player** — one keyboard tile funnels one global engine state; needs a
  key-map field, and the `QS.__engine` back-channel is an N2 smell.
- **Two bullets alive** — genuine edge. One cell = one state; no dynamic
  instantiation; N4 makes spawn/destroy structurally awkward. Embrace: "this
  fabric has exactly one bullet, on purpose."
- **Walk TO a player-chosen target** — NPC targets are written only by their
  own weighted loop; friendX/friendY only bias waving, never movement. A goto
  port would be small but collides with the autonomy premise (half-edge).
- **Pac-Man chasing** — pursuit is a missing tile (seek/steer); maze
  pathfinding is a genuine edge under N1 (you'd hand-wire every wall).
- **Win/lose, ever** — nothing can stop or banner the room; score only counts
  up; `pilot.lives` floors at 0 and the ship sails on. The contract currently
  pretends games don't end.
- **Spawning/collectibles/splitting asteroids** — cleanest edge: the fabric is
  a *fixed set* of cells whose state changes, never a changing set of cells.
  `creatureGrid` fakes multiplicity with a pre-declared alive[] mask.
- **Enemies that shoot back** — bullet has no direction; *the canonical
  invaders example itself is missing return fire and player death.*
- **Correct collision for a big sprite** — crash pad (18px) is unrelated to
  anything drawn; visible size and real hitbox diverge invisibly (soft N2).

**Sharpest single finding:** fan-in dead-end; cheapest lawful fix the Mixer.
(Pressure-tested in pass 4 — verdict below.)

## Pass 4 — follow-up (Sonnet 5): pressure-testing the sharpest findings

This is the pass that earned its tokens. It read the engine code and
**disproved part of the premise I gave it:**

- **The fan-in flag is cosmetic.** `addWire` flags the second wire
  `shared-port`, but *nothing in the engine ever reads `w.flag`* —
  `inputsFor` (engine.js:263) loops every wire into the port and overwrites
  `inputs[toPort]`, so the room quietly runs **last-write-wins by wire-creation
  order**. "Flagged, never coerced" is editor paint over an engine that does
  something else. I verified this against `js/engine.js` myself — true.
  Whatever else ships, flagged wires need delivery semantics or refusal.
- **Drop the moded Mixer; ship `Sum` (numbers) and `Either` (events, OR).**
  A mode switch moves the program from the canvas into dropdowns — the
  spreadsheet leaks in. Two single-purpose tiles keep "topology carries all
  the meaning." Also: `latest` is undefinable on one-tick snapshots (no
  timestamps; both inputs are equally old) and non-associative chains are a
  visual accident a kid can't read. Auto-merge in the engine is worse — it
  picks semantics for the kid and hides arithmetic in a wire with no face.
  Middle path: dragging a second wire into an occupied input offers to drop a
  `Sum`/`Either` tile onto the wire.
- **Endings: defend "games don't end, they transform."** A hard game-over tile
  would be the first tile that freezes its peers (N3 violation) and kills the
  inspect-while-running loop. Ship a pure **Threshold tile** (value in;
  crossed/held out; all fields visible, latches, arms on event) — as lawful as
  scorePing. The ending is whatever the kid wires `crossed` into: creatures
  become butterflies, the keeper sweeps the wreckage. "Systems don't halt,
  they change regime" is a truer lesson than game-over.
- **Month-one danger ranking:** (1) silent no-op wires — a solid-looking wire
  that does nothing poisons trust fastest; `swapTile` doesn't even check port
  *type* on keep-by-name; (2) fan-in, worse than claimed (see above); (3)
  endings — real problem, slow fuse, surfaces week 3-4.

## The dream level — NET-CAST, composite (my synthesis, Pass 3 + Pass 1 #18 + my wiring)

**Story:** Stars fall on a quiet harbor. You are the net. Wire yourself to the
sky; catch ten; the sky goes calm — and the lighthouse keeper waves.

**Wiring (all real ports):**
- keyboard.move → ship.move
- ship.x → crash.bulletX; ship.y → crash.bulletY (the ship IS the catcher)
- constant(1).out → crash.bulletAlive *(small addition: Constant, 1 field)*
- grid.grid → crash.grid
- crash.killIndex → grid.killIndex (a caught star leaves the sky — collection
  without a collect tile)
- crash.boom → ring.boom; crash.boom → score.boom (fan-out: same event, the
  look AND the count)
- crash.boom → counter.bump *(small addition: Counter-with-threshold, 2 fields)*
- counter.hit → grid.reset (the tenth catch empties the sky — the ending)
- keeper.x → pilot.friendX; keeper.y → pilot.friendY — wait, correction: the
  keeper is an NPC; the friend wires go **pilot.x → keeper.friendX,
  pilot.y → keeper.friendY**, and **keeper.hello → counter.arm** is *not* a
  real port — my first draft of this paragraph wired a non-port and is kept
  crossed-out here on purpose, because the honest wiring is simpler:
  **grid.grid → pilot.grid; pilot.wave → grid.reset** already exists in
  harbor, and in NET-CAST it becomes the true emergent channel (below).

**The 60 seconds (Haiku's script, kept):** 0s empty room, stars falling; 5s
first wire, key moves ship; 15-35s position wires, the crash reticle follows;
35s the Constant — "that 1 means the net is ready"; 40s first catch, ring,
score 1; 60s the kid says the sentence that matters: "the whole game is just
numbers moving."

**The real swap moment (my substitution):** at catch #5 the teacher swaps the
**Boom: Ring tile for Boom: Sparks mid-game** — same wire, different sky. Then
swaps the keeper's **Personality card Grumpy → Helpful** and her whole idle
loop rearranges (cWave overtakes cTend in the inspector). Two swaps, both
surviving by port name (M2) — wiring never changes.

**The emergence nobody specced (my pick, from Pass 1 families):** the pilot's
`wave` output fires both on breach and on a clear-field — wire `pilot.wave →
grid.reset` and the sky quietly refills *because you're winning too well*.
Ten catches empties the sky; the pilot's 120-tick all-clear then calls a fresh
wave while the counter holds your 10. Net effect: a game that keeps giving
itself a second act with no ending tile anywhere — the "transform, don't end"
identity, arriving as wiring, not as a rule. The kid finds it by watching
`pilot.clearFor` climb while the sky is empty.

**The ending:** counter crosses 10 → `hit` event → grid.reset fires with
nothing left to reset. The score tile reads 10 forever, the sea is calm, the
keeper waves (she's on Helpful). A number a kid can see, not an engine stop.

## Contract holes (M/N laws strained by real game ideas)

1. **M3 vs. endings.** "The room keeps running" has no answer for "the game
   is over." Verdict: not a hole to patch but an identity to claim
   (transform-don't-end), with a lawful Threshold tile as the fuse. One
   sentence if codified: *a tile may render a room-wide overlay on a routed
   event, provided it is dismissable and mutates no state but its own.*
2. **Fan-in law is a fiction as implemented.** The contract says flagged,
   never coerced; the engine delivers last-write-wins silently (verified:
   `w.flag` is never read). The law must either become real (flagged wires
   deliver nothing) or the palette must own combining (`Sum`/`Either` tiles).
3. **N2 spirit vs. silent no-ops.** Legal wires that receivers ignore (skin
   on rocket; personality keys remapped on the pilot). M2 keep-by-port-name
   actively misleads when the same key means different verbs. Needs: a
   receiving face that shows "I heard a value I don't use," or a port-level
   compatibility check on swap.
4. **N2 vs. silent clamping.** The actor clamps at bounds and emits nothing;
   "I hit the ground" is unobservable. Landing, walls, exits — all strain.
5. **N1 spirit vs. the keyboard back-channel.** `QS.__engine` is a hidden
   global input source the inspector doesn't own.
6. **N4 / fixed-cell-set vs. multiplicity.** No spawn, no destroy, ever. The
   sanctioned shape is fixed-N masks (creatureGrid). Keep as edge; say it out
   loud in the docs — "exactly one bullet, on purpose" is marketing *and* law.
7. **Soft N2: hitbox honesty.** crash pad=18 is unrelated to drawn size;
   the hitbox should be a routed value like the skin slot.
